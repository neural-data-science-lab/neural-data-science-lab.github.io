Justus is a doctoral researcher at ScaDS.AI Dresden/Leipzig, working in cooperation with the Max Planck Institute for Human Cognitive and Brain Sciences. He builds and probes foundation models for neuroimaging, asking two questions: how can we learn useful representations of brain data without costly labels, and how can we trust what those models have learned?

His recent work looks inside EEG foundation models. In "From Clever Hans to Scientific Discovery" ([arXiv:2605.11885](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11885)), he applies attention-aware Layer-wise Relevance Propagation to Transformer-based EEG models. The method exposes shortcut learning, such as models relying on eye-movement artifacts instead of motor signals. It also surfaces a candidate sensorimotor signature of emotional arousal, a hypothesis for neuroscientists to test.

His broader interests are self-supervised learning on neuroimaging data, explainability, and multimodal alignment and fusion.

Justus has participated in research since his Bachelor's in Jena, where his thesis explored Transformers for data imputation. He later worked as a student researcher on explainable EEG foundation models and previously as a working student in software engineering at Salesforce. He also studied abroad at the University of Crete and is passionate about volunteering in the international community.

## Student projects and supervision

Justus is happy to hear from Bachelor's and Master's students who want to work on interpretable machine learning for neuroimaging data. Projects are co-supervised with Dr. Nico Scherf (Max Planck Institute for Human Cognitive and Brain Sciences) and Prof. Bogdan Franczyk (Faculty of Economics, Leipzig University). Possible directions include:

- **Making explanations usable for neuroscientists.** EEG experts think in frequency bands, but foundation models work on raw signals. Translate attribution maps into the frequency domain, or build interactive tools for inspecting individual model decisions.
- **Testing whether explanations can be trusted.** Compare attribution methods on EEG foundation models with faithfulness measures, on simulated and real data.
- **Hunting for shortcuts.** Check whether popular EEG benchmarks and models rely on artifacts (eye movements, muscle activity, heartbeat) rather than brain activity.
- **Self-supervised pretraining for EEG.** Explore alternatives to masked-autoencoding objectives, for example joint-embedding predictive approaches, which are still underexplored for EEG.

**You might be a good fit if** you are comfortable with Python and PyTorch, know the basics of deep learning, and are curious about how models "think", and possibly have a neuroscience background. Not all of these are necessary.

Students from information systems or business informatics are also welcome to propose simulation-based digital transformation case studies, for example modelling an open-source campus or student management system.
